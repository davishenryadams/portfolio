About Me

Mechanical engineer who writes PyTorch. Weird combination. Useful one.

I am a Mechanical Engineering student at Cornell University, graduating May 2027 with a minor in Business. My coursework covers the full core of ME: statics, dynamics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, propulsion, mechanical synthesis, and system dynamics. But somewhere along the way I got deep into machine learning and never looked back.

That combination is something I lean into. At Apple, I was an AIML Intern where I built graph-based recurrent neural networks, trained GANs on internal datasets, and developed Boids-based multi-agent simulations to evaluate learned model behavior. The work hit roughly 96% validation accuracy on internal datasets. It was the kind of role where the physics of how things move and the ML needed to model that motion were equally important.

At Princeton, I did undergraduate research on computational models of collective animal herd motion. That meant preprocessing tens of thousands of aerial wildlife tracking trajectories, building behavior-inspired neural networks, and predicting zebra herd movement up to 5 seconds ahead for groups of 5 to 10 interacting agents. Genuinely one of the most interesting problems I have worked on.

On campus, I am a Sub-Team Lead for Design Build Fly, where we design, build, and race custom RC aircraft in the AIAA national competition. I redesigned the aircraft control surfaces from a pin hinge system to an Internal Drive System, cutting cost by 47%, and used XFLR5 to find an optimal airfoil that pushed our speed up 22%.

Outside of engineering I play club basketball, serve as a Cornell Tradition Fellow, am a brother of Iota Phi of Kappa Alpha Psi, and chair Conference Planning and Community Service for NSBE at Cornell. Building community is important to me.
