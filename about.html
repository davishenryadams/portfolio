About

Mechanical engineer with applied expertise in machine learning, physical simulation, and autonomous systems.

Davis Adams is a Mechanical Engineering student at Cornell University, graduating May 2027 with a minor in Business. Academic foundation spans the core of mechanical engineering — statics, dynamics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, propulsion, mechanical synthesis, and system dynamics — complemented by deep technical work in machine learning and applied artificial intelligence.

At Apple, Davis served as an AIML Intern where he developed graph-based recurrent neural networks, trained generative adversarial networks on internal datasets, and engineered Boids-based multi-agent simulations to evaluate emergent model behavior. The work achieved approximately 96% validation accuracy on internal benchmarks, operating at the intersection of physical motion modeling and large-scale learned inference.

As an undergraduate researcher at Princeton University, Davis contributed to computational modeling of collective animal locomotion. The project involved preprocessing tens of thousands of aerial wildlife tracking trajectories, designing behavior-inspired neural architectures, and producing multi-step predictions of zebra herd dynamics up to five seconds ahead for groups of five to ten interacting agents.

On campus, Davis serves as a Sub-Team Lead for Design Build Fly, directing the design and fabrication of competitive aircraft for the AIAA national competition. His redesign of aircraft control surfaces from a pin hinge system to an Internal Drive System reduced manufacturing cost by 47%, and aerodynamic optimization through XFLR5 delivered a 22% improvement in flight speed.

Beyond engineering, Davis is a member of CU Club Basketball, a Cornell Tradition Fellow, a brother of Iota Phi Chapter of Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity, Inc., and serves as Conference Planning and Community Service Chair for NSBE at Cornell — a role through which he has organized regional conferences and outreach programming engaging hundreds of students across multiple institutions.
